1. Introduction

1.1 Overview

The Smart Agriculture Management System is a web-based platform for the farmlands of Tamil Nadu. It brings live soil and land telemetry, irrigation-dam storage information, localised agronomic advice, a fertigation (fertiliser and water) calculator, government scheme information and market prices into one place, so that a farmer can take decisions from a phone or computer instead of depending on guesswork. The platform is organised around the seven agro-climatic zones of the State and the traditional Pattam (seasonal) calendar, and it is designed to work with low-cost field devices: multi-depth soil moisture probes, optical NPK sensors, solenoid drip valves and a LoRaWAN gateway.

Because farm details, land information and sensor controls are sensitive, the system uses role-based access with two kinds of signed-in users: farmers (customers) and the creator administrator. A visitor who has not signed in can read the public pages only. The application is deployed on Vercel and can be opened from any modern browser.

The system follows a client–server architecture. The responsive web front end talks to a Node.js and Express REST API, and the data (users, farmland telemetry, reviews, enquiries) is stored in a JSON file store. The front end is hosted on Vercel and the API on Render. The project is documented using OOASE practices: requirements prepared in MS Word, seven UML diagrams, implementation in VS Code, API testing with Postman and version control with Git.

1.2 Problem Statement

Most small and marginal farmers in Tamil Nadu still manage water and fertiliser by experience and by the calendar alone. This leads to recurring problems:

  • Erratic water supply – monsoon rainfall is uneven and Cauvery basin water is scarce, yet irrigation is started and stopped by hand.
  • Over- and under-watering – without soil moisture data, crops such as paddy, coconut and turmeric receive too much or too little water.
  • Unscientific fertiliser use – urea, DAP and potash are applied by habit rather than by soil nutrient levels, raising cost and damaging soil.
  • No real-time soil information – nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, pH and micronutrient status are known only after a laboratory test.
  • Scattered advice – crop calendars, zone details, dam levels, market prices and subsidy schemes are spread across many offices and portals.
  • Missed subsidies – eligible farmers often do not know about micro-irrigation, solar pump and crop-insurance schemes.
  • Manual records – farmer details, landholdings and feedback are kept on paper and are hard to search or report.

1.3 Objectives

  • Provide a live land report showing soil and field telemetry together with the storage, inflow and outflow of major irrigation dams.
  • Let farmers register, sign in, maintain a profile with photo, district, landholding and primary crops, and accept the terms and conditions.
  • Give crop-wise fertigation prescriptions (urea, DAP, MOP, bio-fertiliser, zinc sulphate, daily water and drip run-time) from crop, acreage, growth phase and soil texture.
  • Publish an agri-tech guide covering IoT probe installation, drip rules, NPK and pH thresholds, agro-climatic zones, the Pattam calendar and the nutrient deficiency matrix.
  • Present Tamil Nadu government welfare schemes and subsidies, and live market (AGMARKNET) prices for important crops.
  • Collect farmer reviews and support enquiries, and show project revenue and the customer directory.
  • Restrict administrative functions strictly to the creator administrator and secure the system with login, role-based access and encrypted passwords.
  • Model the system with standard UML diagrams and implement it with a layered, maintainable code base.

1.4 Scope of the Project

The system covers the cycle from farmer registration to advisory: the farmer registers and signs in, views the live land report and market prices, studies the guide, runs the fertigation calculator, reads about schemes and subsidies, and submits reviews or support enquiries; the administrator reviews customers, revenue and platform telemetry.

Included: registration and login, role management, farmer profile with photo, live land report, dam storage radar, farmlands gallery, agri-tech guide, fertigation calculator, government schemes, market price ticker, project revenue, customer directory, reviews, support desk and administrator console.

Not included: fully commercial IoT hardware integration, real payment gateway for subscriptions, a native mobile application and multi-language (Tamil/English) switching (see Section 6.3).

2.7 Business Rules

  • Only the creator administrator may use the administrative gate; farmer logins are rejected there and customers have no administrative privileges.
  • A farmer must accept the Terms and Conditions before registration is complete.
  • Farmers retain manual physical override rights on their farm valves at all times.
  • An emergency shut-off is triggered automatically if rainfall exceeds 15 mm; scheduled cycles hold if more than 10 mm is forecast within 12 hours.
  • Drip actuation follows soil texture: sandy loam starts below 55% moisture and stops at 82%; coconut rings start below 50% field capacity; acidic hill soil suppresses irrigation in morning mist or humidity above 85%.
  • Fertigation quantities are calculated per acre of the selected crop and growth phase and scaled by the land area entered.
  • Land coordinates and survey numbers are encrypted and not shared with unauthorised third parties.
  • A deactivated user can no longer log in, but historical reviews and records are kept.

2.8 Feasibility Study

  • Technical: all software is open source or free-tier, well documented and needs only JavaScript skills and ordinary hardware.
  • Economic: there is no licence cost; Vercel and Render free tiers are enough, and the JSON file store needs no database licence.
  • Operational: quick demo accounts and a one-click creator verification make evaluation easy for examiners.

4. Project Coding

4.1 Architecture Overview

The system is a three-tier application. The presentation tier is the browser interface built with HTML, CSS and JavaScript; the application tier is a Node.js and Express REST API that validates requests and applies the business rules; the data tier is the JSON file store (backend/data/db.json) accessed through the Node.js file system module. Client and server communicate only through JSON over HTTP.

A request flows as follows: the page calls the API and attaches the login token; Express applies the security middleware and routes the request to the matching route module; the authenticate middleware checks the token and role; the handler reads or writes db.json; and the JSON result is rendered in the page.

The key business logic is the fertigation service. Per-acre norms for the chosen crop and phase are multiplied by the land area; for example, for 5 acres of Samba/Kuruvai paddy in the vegetative stage the result is 275 kg urea, 200 kg DAP, 125 kg MOP, 20 bio-fertiliser packets and 57,500 litres of water per day with a 90-minute drip cycle. 

function prescribe(crop, acres, phase, soil) {
  const n = NORMS[crop][phase]; // per-acre norms
  return {
    ureaKg: n.urea * acres,
    dapKg: n.dap * acres,
    mopKg: n.mop * acres,
    bioPackets: n.bio * acres,
    zincKgPerAcre: n.zinc,
    waterLitresPerDay: n.water[soil] * acres,
    dripMinutes: n.runtime
  };
}

The irrigation rule engine decides valve actions from soil moisture and rainfall:

function decide(reading, rule, rain) {
  if (rain.mm > 15) return 'EMERGENCY_SHUTOFF';
  if (rain.forecastMm12h > 10) return 'HOLD';
  if (reading.moisture < rule.startBelow) return 'OPEN';
  if (reading.moisture >= rule.stopAt) return 'CLOSE';
  return 'NO_CHANGE';
}

6. Conclusion and References

6.1 Conclusion

The Smart Agriculture Management System shows how object-oriented analysis and modern web technology can support farmers of Tamil Nadu. The requirement analysis identified the user roles and a set of functional and non-functional requirements; the UML diagrams translated them into a clear design (use case for functions, class for data, sequence, activity and state chart for behaviour, component and deployment for architecture); and the design was implemented as a responsive web front end, a Node.js and Express REST API and a JSON file data store.

The finished system lets farmers view live land and dam information, plan fertiliser and water with the fertigation calculator, learn from the guide, discover subsidies and share feedback, while the creator administrator controls the platform through a restricted gate. Security rests on hashed passwords, token authentication, role-based access and origin control, and the work was verified with Postman and test scenarios. The project also gave practical experience of the complete software engineering cycle.

6.2 Limitations

  • Sensor data come from a built-in simulator, and dam levels and market prices are demonstration values; there is no live integration with official sources or hardware in this version.
  • Data are kept in a JSON file store, which suits a demonstration but not heavy concurrent use.
  • No online payment for subscriptions.
  • The interface is a responsive web application; there is no native mobile app.
  • Notifications are shown in the application only; SMS alerts for motor trips and dry runs are designed but not implemented.
  • The interface language is English only.

6.3 Future Enhancements

  • Live integration with LoRaWAN sensors, solenoid valves and satellite NDVI crop-health maps in place of the simulator.
  • Migration of the JSON store to a database such as MongoDB or PostgreSQL.
  • Official data feeds for dams (PWD) and market prices (AGMARKNET).
  • Tamil and English language switching and voice support for farmers.
  • SMS and WhatsApp alerts for irrigation, rainfall and pump faults.
  • Mobile application with offline support for fields with weak networks.
  • Machine-learning yield prediction and pest-disease detection from photographs.
